A laundry routine can involve several scented products, not just detergent. Start with an inventory of a typical wash and dry. This helps you understand your routine—not test whether a product caused a symptom.

## Look at the whole routine

Write down the exact names of your detergent, pretreatment, fabric softener, scent beads, dryer sheets, and fabric sprays. Include products someone else adds to a shared wash. Photograph the packaging for your own reference so you can distinguish similarly named versions.

The [FDA’s fragrance guidance](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/fragrances-cosmetics) lists detergents, softeners, and dryer sheets among products that can contain fragrance. A familiar laundry smell is not a measurement of cleanliness.

## Start with optional scent

Consider leaving out extras whose purpose is to add perfume. If you change detergent, compare an explicitly fragrance-free version and read its current ingredient information. Follow detergent, appliance, and fabric-care instructions.

Do not mix cleaning chemicals or add improvised mixtures. This guide does not prescribe a decontamination method or guarantee that a wash will remove every residue.

## Look beyond the label

A fragrance-free claim helps identify a product to investigate. It does not establish that you will tolerate every other ingredient. “Unscented” may involve masking fragrance. Read our [label guide](../unscented-vs-fragrance-free/) before comparing bottles.

Our [Surcare laundry-liquid profile](../../products/surcare-laundry-liquid/) is a worked example: a manufacturer’s fragrance claim, official product image, and direct brand link. It is not a personal trial or a promise about your skin.

## In a shared laundry

Ask which products are supplied before starting a wash. Discuss a shared approach to detergent and optional additives. Avoid deliberately repeating an exposure to see whether symptoms return.

For continuing symptoms, note what happened and speak with a healthcare professional rather than assuming detergent is the only possible cause. The [self-check](../../self-check/) can help organise observations; it does not diagnose sensitivity.

*Source reviewed: FDA, Fragrances in Cosmetics, accessed 29 September 2026. Routine-planning suggestions are editorial guidance, not a tested treatment protocol.*
