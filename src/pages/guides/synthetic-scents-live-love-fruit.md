## Read the original

[“5 Serious Dangers of Synthetic Scents and How to Protect Yourself” — Live Love Fruit](https://livelovefruit.com/5-dangers-of-synthetic-scents/) discusses respiratory symptoms, ingredient disclosure and broader health concerns. It is a consumer-wellness article, not a clinical guideline or systematic evidence review.

## Keep the evidence in view

Fragrance can cause contact allergy and trigger symptoms in susceptible people. The [FDA’s cosmetic-allergen guidance](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/allergens-cosmetics) explains these reactions and why labels may not identify each scent ingredient.

The source’s broad claims about cancer, organ damage and reproductive effects should not be read as proof that every scented product causes those outcomes. Assess the particular ingredient, exposure and study design. Our [health evidence guide](../fragrance-health-evidence/) and [VOCs and endocrine disruptors explainer](../vocs-and-endocrine-disruptors/) help distinguish those questions.

## A practical starting point

Reduce optional added scents, review ingredient labels and choose replacements category by category with the [starter toolkit](../fragrance-free-starter-toolkit/). Essential oils still add fragrance and can contain allergens; replacing perfume with oils is not strict fragrance avoidance.
