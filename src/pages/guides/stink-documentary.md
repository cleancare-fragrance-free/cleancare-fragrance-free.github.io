## What is *Stink!*?

*Stink!* is a 2015 documentary directed by Jon J. Whelan. The film begins when Whelan tries to identify a strong smell coming from a pair of children’s pajamas. His questions widen into an investigation of product ingredients, fragrance disclosure, chemical regulation, and corporate influence in the United States.

The [official trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ICN52Uzoo0I) describes the film as a consumer journey from a retailer to laboratories, boardrooms, and Congress. [Netflix’s listing](https://www.netflix.com/title/80076581) identifies it as a 2015 documentary and provides availability information, which may differ by country and change over time.

## Why it belongs in this library

The film makes an abstract issue tangible: consumers may see the single word “fragrance” without seeing every ingredient in that mixture. The [US FDA](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/fragrances-cosmetics) confirms that cosmetic fragrance ingredients can generally be declared collectively as “Fragrance,” while manufacturers remain responsible for product safety.

That labeling question matters. It does not mean that every fragrance formula contains the same substances, or that every product causes the same effect at every exposure.

## Watch critically

*Stink!* is advocacy journalism, not a systematic review or clinical study. A compelling story can identify important questions, but each health claim should still be checked against the named chemical, dose, route of exposure, study population, and regulatory context.

Use the film as a starting point, then compare its themes with the [FDA fragrance overview](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/fragrances-cosmetics), [EPA guidance on fragrance indoors](https://www.epa.gov/asthma/can-use-fragrances-indoors-cause-health-impacts), and the studies in our [evidence library](/evidence/).
