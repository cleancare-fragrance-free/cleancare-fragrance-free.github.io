## What is *Toxic Beauty*?

*Toxic Beauty* is a 2019 documentary directed by Phyllis Ellis. The film follows people involved in litigation concerning Johnson & Johnson talc products and examines broader questions about chemicals, ingredient disclosure, and oversight in cosmetics and personal-care products.

The [film’s official site](https://www.toxicbeautydoc.com/the-film) describes interviews with scientists, lawyers, advocates, regulators, survivors, and industry critics. It also follows a participant who tracks selected chemical exposures while changing her personal-care routine.

## Why it belongs in this library

The film encourages viewers to ask useful questions: What is in a product? What does a label disclose? Which claims are supported by human evidence, and which remain uncertain? Those questions also matter when products contain fragrance.

The film is about cosmetics and personal care broadly; it is not solely about fragrance. A claim about talc, parabens, or one exposure route should not be treated as proof that every cosmetic ingredient or every fragranced product presents the same risk.

## Watch critically

*Toxic Beauty* is an advocacy documentary, not a systematic review or clinical guideline. Personal stories can reveal important problems, while still requiring each scientific or legal claim to be checked independently. Look for the named substance, dose, route of exposure, study design, and regulatory context.

For additional context, compare the film with the [US FDA overview of fragrance in cosmetics](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/fragrances-cosmetics), the [FDA explanation of cosmetic safety and regulation](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/cosmetics-us-law), and the studies collected in our [evidence library](/evidence/).
