## What the article covers

The David Suzuki Foundation’s article, [“Toxic ingredient to avoid: Parfum (a.k.a. fragrance)”](https://davidsuzuki.org/living-green/dirty-dozen-parfum-fragrance/), introduces fragrance labeling in cosmetics and personal-care products. It explains that a formula may contain botanical and synthetic materials as well as solvents, stabilizers, dyes, or preservatives, while the label may show only “fragrance” or “parfum.”

It also discusses allergy, sensitivity, indoor-air emissions, environmental pathways, and label terms to look for when trying to avoid scent.

## What is well supported

The labeling limitation is also described by the [US FDA](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/fragrances-cosmetics): individual fragrance ingredients can generally be listed collectively as “Fragrance.” The FDA also notes that some people may be allergic or sensitive to certain fragrance ingredients.

The [American Lung Association](https://www.lung.org/clean-air/indoor-air/indoor-air-pollutants/fragrances) explains that fragranced products can add volatile compounds to indoor air and may trigger symptoms such as irritation, headaches, coughing, or wheezing in some people, especially those with asthma or other sensitivities.

## Where to read cautiously

The Foundation page is an environmental advocacy explainer rather than a clinical guideline. Some statements group many possible fragrance materials and health outcomes together. “May contain a chemical associated with a hazard” is not the same as “this product causes that outcome when used as directed.”

Fragrance formulas, ingredient concentrations, routes of exposure, and individual susceptibility vary. Essential oils are also fragrance mixtures and can cause contact allergy, so replacing synthetic perfume with essential oils is not the same as going fragrance-free.

## A practical takeaway

If you want to reduce exposure, begin with products that intentionally disperse scent into the room, then review laundry, cleaners, leave-on skincare, and hair products. Choose an explicit fragrance-free claim for the exact product and recheck the current ingredient list.
