Start with the products and places you encounter most often. You may not need a new device or a full household replacement. For many indoor air concerns, the first step is to understand and reduce the source.

## 1. Notice the main sources

Look at what is sprayed, diffused, applied to skin, used in laundry, or added to a room to cover odors. The [EPA identifies air fresheners and several household product types as possible VOC sources](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/volatile-organic-compounds-impact-indoor-air-quality). Its [indoor-odor guide](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-09/documents/view_indoors_odors.pdf) recommends limiting products or practices that cause odor problems, especially in shared spaces.

## 2. Change the source before masking it

If an odor comes from dampness, a spill, or inadequate cleaning, address that cause. Adding a fragrance changes how the room smells; it does not identify or remove the cause. The EPA advises source reduction and appropriate ventilation for indoor pollutants. Follow product directions and never mix household cleaners unless the label specifically directs it.

## 3. Read beyond the front label

“Unscented” may still involve a masking fragrance. For US cosmetics, the FDA says labels such as “fragrance-free” do not have a federal standard that guarantees suitability for someone with an allergy. Check the current ingredient list, product version, and manufacturer information. Our [label guide](../unscented-vs-fragrance-free/) explains the distinction in more detail.

## 4. Consider other people sharing the air

In a shared home, workplace, or vehicle, ask about strong-smelling products before using them. The EPA’s [indoor-odor guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-09/documents/view_indoors_odors.pdf) recommends reducing exposures that may cause problems for others and using adequate ventilation.

## 5. Compare alternatives carefully

An alternative should fit the task and the person using it. “Natural,” “organic,” and “essential oil” do not automatically mean fragrance-free or suitable for a sensitive person. The [products-by-country directory](../../directory/) records official brand sources, country-specific details, and check dates. Profiles cover checked sources in several countries; manufacturer claims are not independent safety tests or a guarantee of individual tolerance.

If you are experiencing persistent symptoms, discuss them with a qualified clinician; a label or website cannot identify a personal trigger.
